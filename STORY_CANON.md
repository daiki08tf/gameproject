# Blade Vale 3.0 — Story Canon

> Phase 11.1 canonical narrative reference. Gameplay code remains the source of truth for progression/unlocks/rewards; this document is the source of truth for **what those systems mean in-world**.

## Central mystery

**なぜBlade Valeの世界と現代世界はつながっているのか？**

The player's long-term objective is not simply to defeat a final evil. The journey begins as a regional adventure, then becomes an investigation into why boundaries are failing, who built or maintained them, and why an unknown observer treats whole worlds as objects to be measured and selected.

The answer is deliberately staged. Early chapters show contradictions and recurring ruins. The Veil arc names the boundary. The outer-world arc reveals a network. Machine World reveals a management/observation layer. The Modern World remains a late mystery revealed first through sensory evidence rather than exposition.

## Cosmology

### Human Realm / 人界

The player's home world. Its peoples normally believe they inhabit an ordinary complete world. In reality it is one world layer within a larger boundary network.

### Heaven / 天界

A neighboring high-order world associated with light, Relics and beings once interpreted as gods. Heaven is powerful, but it is **not** the absolute creator layer. Old records of judgment and intervention reflect contact between world layers.

### Underworld / 冥界

Another neighboring layer where remnants of memory, death and unstable existence accumulate. It is not simply an afterlife. Veil damage allows residues and creatures to cross between it and other worlds.

### Boundary Layer / 境界層

The infrastructure between worlds: corridors, seals, observation stations, gates and isolation zones. Key Dungeons, rifts and the Boundary Throne belong to this family.

### Machine World / 機界

An artificial world layer involved in operating, recording and judging parts of the boundary network. MOTHER and ARCHITECT systems behave like administrators, but the deepest Machine World records reveal that they too are observed by something outside their authority.

### Modern World / 現代世界

A civilization outside Blade Vale's normal known layers. Its first evidence should be indirect: regular city lights, rail-like vibrations, unknown signage, date fragments, communication signals and handheld devices. Explicit naming such as Tokyo should come only after the player has accumulated enough evidence.

## The Veil

**The Veil is a network, not a single wall.**

It separates world layers, seals dangerous connections, opens controlled routes and allows observation. The shutdown of Black Iron Machine Castle destabilized already-weakened maintenance structures, causing old routes and fractures to reactivate.

The seven-key system belongs to the known control architecture. The Eighth Key is abnormal: it accesses routes outside the accepted seven-key structure and leads toward Machine World and external observation layers.

Some monsters previously treated as enemies were actually guardians. The Primordial Beast Abyss is the clearest example: it was holding a fracture closed from inside.

## Story arcs

### Arc I — Ch1–15: 地上の旅

A recognizable RPG journey first. The player encounters regional threats, bosses, ruins and cultures. Small anomalies recur across distant areas: identical symbols, impossible machinery and structures older than local history. Black Iron Machine Castle is the turning point where these clues stop looking accidental.

**Reveal:** the world has seams.

### Arc II — Ch16–20: The Veil

Lost cities, beings from above, material from outside the world and broken time/space make the boundary explicit. Ch19 finally names The Veil. Ch20 reveals that the defeated final guardian was sealing the fracture.

**Reveal:** the boundary is failing.

### Arc III — Ch21–25: 外縁世界

The player explores connected outer layers and eventually reaches the Boundary Throne. These regions are not simply continents beyond the map: they demonstrate that Blade Vale is one node in a much larger boundary network.

**Reveal:** the world is part of a system.

### Arc IV — 第八鍵 / 機界

The abnormal Eighth Key reaches an artificial world. Machine systems call worlds observation targets or experimental layers. The player initially assumes Machine World built the system; deeper records overturn that assumption.

**Reveal:** the administrators are also being observed.

### Arc V — 現代世界の信号

Signals beyond Machine World begin matching a highly structured civilization. The story confirms that a Modern World exists before explaining why it connects to Blade Vale.

**Reveal:** the connection is real; the reason remains the central mystery.

## Existing systems in-world

| System | Narrative meaning |
|---|---|
| Abyss | Damaged Veil space filled with mixed laws, residue and fragments from world layers. |
| World Tier | Rising boundary pressure changes how the same region manifests. |
| Nemesis | An enemy that absorbed a combat imprint/resonance from the player's defeat. |
| Secret Realm | A small isolated world fragment, failed connection or sealed sector whose coordinates become temporarily fixed. |
| Key Dungeon | A maintenance/access route through the boundary network. |
| Unique Trial | A weapon/equipment memory condition left by a previous owner, creator or local world law. |
| Raid | A boundary-scale threat re-observed in a more dangerous state, not merely a boss with more HP. |
| Machine World | Artificial administration/observation layer that ultimately proves it is not the top of the hierarchy. |

## Clue ladder

Story additions should follow this escalation unless a specific reveal has already occurred:

1. **違和感** — repeated symbols, impossible materials, inconsistent histories.
2. **境界の兆候** — spatial distortions, missing cities, Heaven/Underworld interference.
3. **The Veil** — seven hollows/keys, outside-world evidence, guardian truth.
4. **境界網** — outer worlds, Boundary Throne, Eighth Key, experimental-layer terminology.
5. **観測者** — Machine records, external windows, selection/judgment language.
6. **現代世界** — regular lights, train-like sounds, dates, devices, recognizable writing.

## Writing rules

- Story supports exploration and gameplay; Blade Vale does not become a separate visual novel.
- Mandatory text stays compact enough for mobile reading.
- Reveal one useful clue at a time; do not dump cosmology in one scene.
- Give existing mechanics narrative meaning before inventing lore-only systems.
- Preserve meaningful unknown/`?` states until discovery conditions are met.
- Modern-world clues begin as sensory evidence before explicit place names.
- Narrative code must not silently alter level curves, rewards or unlock conditions.
- Boss lines and discoveries should reinforce mechanics where possible: guard roles, Break windows, anomalies, keys and regional rules.
